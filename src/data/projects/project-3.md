  - id: problem
    label: "The Problem"
    title: "Digital banking that feels powerful but not approachable"
    content: |
      <p>Most digital banking apps either feel corporate and cold, or try so hard to look modern that they sacrifice clarity. Users opening a banking app usually have a simple goal: check their balance, move money, or complete a quick task without friction.</p>
      <p>Finto started as a concept exploration to answer: <em>what would a digital banking product look like if it felt confident, calm, and easy to trust on mobile?</em></p>
      <p><strong>Note:</strong> This is a personal product design concept, not a shipped client product. It demonstrates UX thinking, visual craft, and end-to-end design execution in fintech.</p>
    highlights:
      - "Busy interfaces that bury primary account information"
      - "Onboarding and verification flows that feel intimidating"
      - "Inconsistent brand experience between marketing site and mobile app"
      - "Currency and account tools treated as afterthoughts"

  - id: approach
    label: "Approach"
    title: "Mobile-first clarity with a cohesive brand layer"
    content: |
      <p>I structured the concept around three experience pillars: a calm wallet home screen, focused utility flows (currency, verification), and a marketing layer that communicates trust and modern infrastructure.</p>
      <p>Visual language uses deep blues, soft gradients, and high-contrast cards so financial data stays legible while the product still feels contemporary.</p>
    blocks:

      - title: "Marketing that matches the product"

  - id: decisions
    label: "Key Decisions"
    title: "Design choices that shaped the concept"
    decisions:
      - title: "Balance-first hierarchy"
        description: "The home screen leads with total balance and available funds before secondary features or promotions."
        rationale: "Most sessions start with 'how much do I have?' That answer should be immediate."
      - title: "Step-based verification"
        description: "KYC is broken into selfie, ID scans, and short questions as discrete cards in a vertical flow."
        rationale: "Chunking complex compliance into steps reduces drop-off and user anxiety."
      - title: "Unified blue brand system"
        description: "Marketing pages, app UI, and card designs share the same blue gradient family and rounded card patterns."
        rationale: "Fintech trust is partly visual. Consistency signals a single product, not disconnected screens."
      - title: "Currency tools as a dedicated surface"
        description: "Exchange rates and price alerts get their own focused view rather than buried in settings."
        rationale: "Users checking FX rates want a fast, glanceable screen without navigating account settings."
